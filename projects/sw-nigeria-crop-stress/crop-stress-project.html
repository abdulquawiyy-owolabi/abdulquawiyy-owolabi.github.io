Satellites can detect crop stress as it happens. They can’t yet see it coming.

A detection system for Oyo, Osun and northern Ogun in Nigeria, built with Earth Engine, Python and Sentinel-1 radar. There are no ground labels, so the claims stay modest and the tests are strict.

Open the live map View the code Read the report

The short version

I defined crop stress as an unusually low vegetation index for a 250 m cropland pixel at that time of year, then tried to predict it from rainfall, air dryness, surface heat and Sentinel-1 radar, none of which come from the optical image the label uses.

Climate and radar improve detection of stress in the same 16-day window. Beyond about 16 days there is no demonstrated gain, because vegetation stress is highly persistent: the last satellite image already tells you most of what the next one will.

I report that plainly. A clear negative result on lead time is more useful to a monitoring programme than an optimistic forecast.

The problem

Rain-fed smallholder farms in southwestern Nigeria are exposed to dry spells, yet field-level stress records are scarce. Satellites can fill the gap, but without independent labels it is easy to build a model that predicts an index with a version of itself and report a flattering score.

The design keeps the label and the predictors on separate sources and tests lead time explicitly, so the numbers mean something.

How it works

  1. Label the stressEach pixel’s vegetation index is compared with its own history for the same 16-day window, 2001 to 2020, leaving the tested year out. Below one standard deviation counts as stress.
  2. Add independent predictorsCHIRPS rainfall and drought index, ERA5-Land soil moisture and air dryness, MODIS surface temperature, and Sentinel-1 radar backscatter from one satellite track at a time.
  3. Model against honest baselinesXGBoost, compared with climatology, a drought-index rule, and persistence of the last known state.
  4. Validate across years and spaceTest data are unseen in both time and place, with a buffer between blocks. Differences are tested with a bootstrap that treats years as the independent unit.

Bold results are the ones whose interval excludes zero. One 48-day result (+0.13) is significant on its own, but it is one of about 70 comparisons and is not supported against persistence, so I treat it as noise.

  • At 0 days, climate and radar each add skill. Treat this as a nowcast, since it uses same-window information.
  • At 16 days the estimates are small and positive, but every interval includes zero. A gain is not demonstrated.
  • A model using only NDVI history is statistically no better than persistence. History adds nothing over carrying the last state forward.

What the model relies on

SHAP shows what the model uses, not what causes stress. At 0 days the previous window’s vegetation index dominates, then same-window surface temperature and air dryness, and among radar variables the VH channel. Rainfall and soil-moisture variables each carry little.

At 16 days, heat and air dryness together carry about 37% of the total importance, rainfall and soil moisture about 30%, vegetation history about 18% and radar about 12%. In plain terms: the model mostly says that vegetation was already stressed and that it is hot and dry now.

Where and when it works

  • Skill peaks in April and fades by July.
  • Stress is common in the north and northwest and rare in the south-central band.
  • The best model beats persistence across most of the area, and does worse in a small cluster at the far north, where its results should not be trusted.
  • Skill varies by year: strong in 2016 and 2020, weak in 2017, 2021 and 2024.

Limits, stated plainly

  • The label is a vegetation anomaly, not crop damage. Stress is not yield loss.
  • Many sample points share one climate pixel, so the effective sample is closer to 572 cells than 3,185 points.
  • About a fifth of points sit where two Sentinel-1 tracks overlap and have no radar data.
  • Radar comparisons rest on 10 test years, so intervals are wide. Only the 0-day results are safe to lean on.
