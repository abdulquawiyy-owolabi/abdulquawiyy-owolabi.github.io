12°00'N 08°35'E — KANO STATE, NG

Land suitability for solar farm development in Kano State

Multi-criteria evaluation · Analytic Hierarchy Process
MethodGIS-MCE + AHP
Criteria weighed7 factors
SoftwareArcGIS Pro
Output3-class suitability map

Overview

This project identifies where solar farm development makes the most sense across Kano State — balancing solar potential against terrain, infrastructure access, land cover, and protected-area constraints. The output is a suitability map that developers or planning authorities could use directly to shortlist candidate sites.

Data sources

  • Digital Elevation Model: 30m resolution, OpenTopography
  • Solar radiation: Global Solar Atlas
  • Land cover (2023): ESA WorldCover
  • Road network: Geofabrik
  • Electrical grid: HDX transmission & distribution points
  • Protected areas: World Database on Protected Areas

Methodology

Criteria & weights

All inputs were clipped to the study area and projected to WGS 1984 UTM Zone 31N. Seven criteria were reclassified and combined in a weighted overlay:

CriterionWeightWhat it captures
Solar potential36%Reclassified photovoltaic power output
Grid proximity16%Distance to electrical transmission/distribution
Road proximity16%Distance to road network
Aspect14%Sun-facing slope orientation
Slope8%Flatness suitable for installation
Land cover5%Suitability of existing land use
Elevation5%Elevation effects on solar potential

Protected areas — conservation zones, wildlife reserves, culturally significant sites — were excluded entirely from consideration rather than merely down-weighted.

Results

The weighted overlay was reclassified into three suitability classes — unsuitable, moderately suitable, and highly suitable — then converted from raster to dissolved vector polygons for easy handoff to planning teams. Large areas of Kano State came out highly suitable, driven by strong solar irradiance and flat terrain, with agricultural land and settlement constraints successfully factored out. The methodology is deliberately reusable: the same weighting framework applies across the wider Sahel region with only the input layers swapped.

Where this could go next

  • Add land ownership and acquisition cost as a criterion
  • Incorporate seasonal climate variability rather than a single-period solar dataset
  • Layer in socio-economic factors for community-impact screening
  • Run a sensitivity analysis across alternative weighting schemes
