12°00'N 08°35'E — KANO MUNICIPAL, NG

Spatiotemporal land surface temperature trend analysis, 2013–2024

Landsat 8 time series · statistical trend testing
Time span11 years
Land cover classes6
MethodsMann-Kendall, QR, PCA
PlatformGoogle Earth Engine

Overview

Urban heat island effects are usually easy to see and hard to prove statistically. This project tests whether land surface temperature in Kano Municipal actually trended upward between 2013 and 2024, and whether that trend differs by land cover type — built-up areas, cropland, bareland, shrubland, grassland, and forest — using five separate statistical approaches rather than relying on visual inspection alone.

Data & method

  • Imagery: Landsat 8 TOA reflectance, 2013–2024, processed in Google Earth Engine
  • Land cover: ESA WorldCover 10m, used to place points of interest per land cover class
  • Boundary: FAO GAUL administrative boundaries
  • Analysis: LST calculated per point of interest, then tested with the Mann-Kendall trend test, quantile regression across five percentiles, seasonal decomposition, extreme value analysis, and PCA for spatial pattern structure

Results

The regional signal was flat — until the data was split by season

A basic Mann-Kendall test across the full year found no statistically significant trend for any land cover type (all p-values above 0.05). But a seasonal Kendall test told a different story: cropland, bareland, and forest all showed statistically significant increasing LST trends once seasonal noise was accounted for — bareland most sharply (p = 0.0002, Sen's slope 0.30°C/year).

Extreme temperature thresholds

Grassland recorded the highest 95th-percentile threshold at 41.43°C, while forest stayed coolest at 35.76°C. Extreme high-temperature events clustered consistently between 2019 and 2020 across nearly every land cover class, while extreme lows clustered in late 2015 and 2022–2023 — a pattern consistent enough across unrelated land covers to point to a regional climate driver rather than land-cover-specific effects alone.

Spatial patterns

Forest and cropland ran hottest on average (~31.5°C) and most variable, while grassland stayed coolest (~29.5°C). All land cover types correlated strongly with each other (0.86–0.98), and a two-component PCA explained roughly 99% of the variance — meaning temperature across this landscape behaves as one tightly coupled system rather than several independent microclimates.

Why it matters

The headline finding isn't "Kano is warming" — the full-year test doesn't support that claim. It's that the trend only appears once you control for seasonality, and even then it concentrates in bareland, forest, and cropland rather than the built environment. That's a more actionable, more defensible result for urban planning and agricultural risk assessment than a blanket warming narrative would be, and it's the kind of nuance that naive before/after imagery comparisons miss entirely.
