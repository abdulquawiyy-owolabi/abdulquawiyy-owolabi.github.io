Satellite image classification using Random Forest and SVM

Land cover mapping · Sentinel-2 imagery
Classes6 land cover types
ModelsRandom Forest, SVM
Accuracy82% (both models)
Bands usedB2, B3, B4, B8, B11, B12

Overview

This project classifies land cover across Iwo Local Government, Osun State, from Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery — water bodies, trees, crops, built-up areas, bareland, and rangeland — to support agricultural monitoring and urban planning decisions. Beyond running the classifiers, the focus was on understanding which spectral bands actually separate these classes and why.

Method

Preprocessing & exploratory analysis

Sentinel-2 bands were loaded and clipped to the study area with rasterio, then used to calculate NDVI and NDMI. True-colour and false-colour composites were built to visually separate the urban core from surrounding vegetation before any modelling started.

Which bands actually separate the classes

Band-by-band histogram analysis showed each Sentinel-2 band carries different discriminating power. B8 (near-infrared) produced the most dramatic separation between classes, reaching pixel values up to 4,500 units and clearly distinguishing vegetation from built and bare surfaces. B4 (red) showed strong separation up to 3,500 units, while B2 (blue) was most sensitive specifically to tree cover. Water bodies consistently registered lower readings than vegetation across nearly every band, though the size of that gap shifted band to band — the core reason multispectral analysis outperforms any single band.

Models & results

Random Forest and SVM classifiers were both evaluated on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, with confusion matrices to trace exactly where misclassification happened.

Both models landed at 82% overall accuracy, but the class-level breakdown tells the real story: performance tracked sample size almost exactly. Trees, with 272 training samples, classified well under both models. Water bodies and crops, with just 2 and 7 samples respectively, were essentially unlearnable — SVM at least achieved perfect precision on water despite the scarcity, while Random Forest failed to identify it at all. This is a textbook class-imbalance problem rather than a modelling failure, and the fix is more balanced ground-truth sampling, not a different algorithm.

Why it matters

82% accuracy with a diagnosed, explainable failure mode is more useful to a client than a higher accuracy number with no explanation of where it breaks. Anyone building on this classification — for crop monitoring or urban growth tracking — knows exactly which classes to trust and which need better ground-truth data before the outputs are used for decisions.
